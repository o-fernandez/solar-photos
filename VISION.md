# Vision & Philosophy

## What this is

A local-first photo library manager in the spirit of Picasa: fast, opinionated,
and respectful of the person using it. It runs on your machine, reads your photos
from your drives, and never requires an account, a subscription, or a cloud.

## Who it's for

People who want to own their photos. Concretely:

- The **"I don't want my data on Google or iCloud"** crowd.
- The **"I won't pay monthly for storage when I have a backup hard drive"** crowd.
- The **"I just want to own my photos"** crowd.

These people are technical enough to tolerate a rough edge if the core is solid,
vocal enough to give real feedback, and loyal to tools whose values match theirs.
They are currently underserved, settling for software that fights them.

## What success looks like

Not a billion users. **Roughly 10,000 excited people who genuinely love it** and
tell each other. A focused, like-minded audience — not mass-market breadth.

This is a deliberate choice, not a consolation. A narrow, aligned audience is
*lighter* to serve than a broad, divergent one. Modest ambition is a feature: it
keeps the maintenance load sane and the product coherent.

## The floor

The first user is the person building it. This tool must be good enough that its
own maker reaches for it daily. If daily personal use is the floor, the project
cannot fail below it — everything above that floor is upside, including the 10,000.

We build it because we want it to exist and want to use it. Users who love it are
the reward that sustains the work, not the oxygen the work depends on.

## What we optimize for

1. **Deference to the user.** The software gets out of the way. It never makes the
   user lose their place. (See PRINCIPLES.md — responsiveness is the product.)
2. **Ownership.** Photos stay where they are, in formats the user controls. No
   lock-in, no phoning home, no account.
3. **Speed at real scale.** Designed for a 100,000+ photo library, not a demo.
4. **Coherence over completeness.** A few things done beautifully beats many things
   done adequately.

## How we decide what NOT to build

When a request arrives, ask: *does this serve the person who wants to own their
photos, without breaking deference, ownership, speed, or coherence?* If it adds
features at the cost of those, the answer is no — even if some users ask for it,
even from a well-meaning contributor.

Staying opinionated is the hard part. Most projects in this space died by saying
yes to everyone. Saying no is how this one stays good.

## Explicitly out of scope (for now)

RAW formats, cloud sync, plugin systems, video (v1), and anything that turns this
into a full darkroom or a competitor to professional cataloging suites. These
aren't rejections forever — they're protected focus for now.
